The Quiet Power of Good Typography

By Maya Rao · 5 minute read

Typography shapes how we read, feel and trust what appears on a screen, often without us noticing it at all. The right typeface can make an article feel calm, serious or playful before a single word is understood.

Choosing the right typeface

Designers usually pair a serif font for long reading with a sans-serif font for headings and interface elements. Serifs guide the eye along a line, while sans-serifs stay crisp at small sizes. Keep it to two or three font families per page for a balanced look.

Spacing makes text readable

Comfortable line-height (around 1.6 for body text), gentle letter-spacing in headings, and a line length near 70 characters help readers move through paragraphs without fatigue. Left-aligned text is usually easiest to read; centered text works best for short headlines.

Good typography is invisible; bad typography is everywhere.

Weight and size create hierarchy

Heavier weights and larger sizes tell readers what matters most. Use bold sparingly, reserve highlights for key ideas, and let italics add a quiet emphasis.
